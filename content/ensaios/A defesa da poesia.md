###### Literature in English: Literary Paradigms

Primarily for an artwork to withstand the passage of time it has to be preserved by people. There is a collective, conscious active decision that humanity has taken to shield from material deterioration artworks that are typically representative of specific cultural-historical movements, as well as social ontological and technological evolution.  As if attempting to build an accordion that unfolds into a perceivable illustration of our metaphysical progress on this planet, we dedicated institutions solely to the purpose of archiving, exhibiting and ensuring these artworks can last for entire centuries, exceeding our very own being and all of those to come. But preservation is not substantiated only by tradition and cultural relevance, if an artwork is fixed and retained for eternity it is because it has been deemed as crucial for the existence of each and every single one of us. It is because the artworks itself self-sustaines its own pertinence over and over again.

So what exactly constitutes an eternal artwork? And how can we define an artwork as eternal or worthy of eternity? The usage of the term classic or masterpiece is solely ascribed to a particular finite sum of works that have become a fundamental constituent to our development as a humanistic, progressive, ethnocultural and expanding society. It characterizes a creation as simultaneously contemporary and timeless, ubiquitous and preponderant. 

Giorgio Agamben’s essay, What is the Contemporary? explains that “ Those who are truly contemporary, who truly belong to their time, are those who neither perfectly coincide with it nor adjust themselves to its demands.” (2008) To identify a contemporary author, we need to search for an indispensable disjunction, an anachronism, that allows the author's corpus to be outside of its own saeculum, the integral historical period in which one belongs to. Agamben goes on to conceptualize that for the poet, to have a firm notion of his contemporary he must consider it not as possible or accessible, but as an indefinable “darkness” that when directly confronted will urge, press and transform temporality. Thus, this conception of “darkness” simply put is an implicit, sticky, irremovable essence integrated into a work of art, or in other words, a pattern we can expect to distinguish naturally from afar.

The reason why the collective tends to single out authors like Shakespeare, Milton and Marvell more than Sidney or Moore is rooted in the simple need that one has to correlate itself to others, to correlate directly with sentiency itself. Human kind is more susceptible to plain sincere representation of the human condition. Our attraction grows with each empirical portrayal of intense, almost unbearable emotions, together with depicted themes of death, grief, hate, sorrow, obsession. Oneself’s capacity of conjuring familiar sensations common to us all, those which are inherent to the function of a beating heart, allow us to reach a continuous unlimited connection beyond any fabricated reality.  

Bearing this in mind, An Apology for Poetry (Sidney, 1595) is a text that could be considered significantly obsolete. A defense of poetry saturated with repetitive claims and excessive references, that leaves the impression of an obstinate writer's attempt to consolidate an irrefutable argument. The unnecessary weighting between poets and historians, musicians, philosophers, grammarians, rhetoricians, physicians, arithmeticians and geometricians (and presumably any other area of expertise) weakens the argument since it’s not by proving superiority that it'll increase the merit of Poetry. It constructs a defense devoid of criticism that lacks an analytical opposing point, a statement reinforced more by examples than by theoretical depth. Furthermore, its substantial composition does not fit our contemporaneity. While the recurrent themes of morality, enlightenment, virtue and devotion are insufficient to persuade a modern reader, Sidney's dependence on specific cultural and traditional values subsequently confine An Apology for Poetry to its own period. 


For Sidney, Poetry is the unparalleled noble teacher, the elite preacher. Advocating for it as the source of knowledge and the guider of men aiming for goodness, divinity, the one who “imitates the inconceivable excellencies of God”. On the contrary this inference reduces the art to one singular purpose of teaching and enlightening. What remains is a seemingly elitist, conceited conviction, disproportionately enlarged to raise the reputation of the Poet as an unmatched, glorified, prophet who is capable of surpassing even Nature’s doings, the only whose deserving of recognition when it comes to instructing “so that the end of all earthly learning being virtuous action.” The bright light that will perfect and correct humanity's degeneration, bringing forth only what may or should be.

Citing C.S. Lewis (1970) in his essay English Literature In The Sixteenth Century Excluding Drama, When referring to Sidney’s work he explains how his aim was ethical and aesthetic, to teach by pleasing. How as an author Sidney “assumes in his readers an agreed response to certain ideals of virtue, honour, friendship, and magnanimity.” He also elaborates on how “The poets of that age were full of reverence - for God, for kings, for fathers, for authority - but not of our reverence for the actual.” 

But if Poetry was to remain attached to these ideas, would it not become a doctrine, more similar to a method or an imperative endeavour. To the utmost degree it would constrict and discipline. His own defense against Plato’s prohibition of poets is provided by the understanding that poetry can be abused for wrong, in this case themes of vanity and lust, unworthy subjects. Supposing poets have the competence to ascertain depraved opinions in man and therefore should not be permitted in society, then the poets whose intentions are the opposite should suffer from the same penalty, since moving men to a purist or absolutist morality would lead to an unreasonable condemnation of those who are not ideologically, ethically or religiously aligned. The unambiguous conception of Poetry as either dishonorable or illustrious robs it of its free expressive nature. Poetry or any other art would not progress if they were meant to be clean and dutiful.

Were I to choose a work as most compelling, An Apology for Poetry would not be my first option. And yet, there is a reason why I believe Sidney’s An Apology for Poetry cannot be overlooked, or disregarded as outdated. Regardless if it focuses on customary principles, it’s the mere existence of such a work that should be well esteemed. Sidney’s effort to prove the unmistakable influence art can have on our cognitive behaviour, defending it as determinant to the function of humanity, contributes to making An Apology for Poetry more significant than its presupposed. We should then reassess the contemporarity of An Apology for Poetry through the main concern it has of establishing art as indispensable. Given that, we can proceed to focus on where to find the evidence and sensibility in Sidney’s words, the fragments still applicable to assemble what should be a current defense of Poetry. 

Taking into account the observations already made by Sidney, Poetry is an art that cannot but be loved, that cannot but affect the people. In addition to being a versatile medium, it’s an art fitted for the memory, accessible to all. Zealous “food for the tenderest stomachs”. Verses can be epic, sacred, erotic, lyrical, idyllic, allegorical or figurative. Created for prophecies, psalms or praise with a prominent role in devotion and frequently held in reverence even by those who are illiterate. It’s a vocation that does not wither even in periods of unenlightenment, a structured poem is always set so not even one word can be lost. 

But more than that, Sidney sees it as a cure, a glue that can bring together a reconciled society, a guide for agreement, unity, and community. That is the most powerful idea of Poetry Sidney can produce: Poetry as an instrument that can lead the heart upon action. 

A poet's job is then to make, partially to incite imagination and partially to gather in our mind the will to act. That is how Poetry advanced to be used as a substitute to physical manifestations of protests. Seen as a political weapon by abolitionists, suffragettes, the working class not only would evoke awareness of injustice, it would create a kindredness with the reader, who could better recognize the perspective of the other’s voice. Authors who understood that empathy could be achieved with description of grief, loss, despair, pain. The same way Sidney thought Poetry could move others, it started to contribute to the divulgation of humanitarian causes. Encouraging people towards change, painting a new way to inhabit the world, shaping a new frame of consciousness. 

A cultural form of resistance is as essential as any other. Poetry is affordable, adaptable, memorable, it can be performed collectively, it spreads easily. Reinvented for political reflection, meant to be recited or sung aloud, articulated for themes of war, love, homeland and separation. Sentimentalism is a political tool that generates outrage, it mobilises and while most of these poems are born in the resistance of the public, with no trace to a specific origin, it still gives voice to the voiceless. There is a feeling of Poetry in all, and mankind is the one exercising it. Especially now in times of genocide there is an overwhelming amount of voices that await to be heard, embedded with everlasting incessant ideas, related to love, unity, faith, nature to further nourish the relationship between people. Art has the chore to keep us together in our humanity, to shift our attitudes. 
  
In final analysis, T.S. Eliot (1919) writes in his essay Tradition and the Individual Talent, how it is of extreme importance for an artist to be aware of the works that precedes them. In relation to literature, he justifies that it is by perceiving the past that the writer becomes acutely aware of his own place in time. When creating a literary work, it needs to be regarded not as a linear addition to the previous queue of works but as a synchronous component integrated in a framework. But what Eliot does not mention is how likewise for a reader, a literary text is not singular or detached from its precedents, it converges, intersects, connects itself to society and the individual in a way that is always applicable, reusable, durable. It can last a lifetime because of what it’s carrying in itself, be it thought, feeling, fear or faith. It’s meant to consolidate within the woven fabric of consciousness enriching the collective and individual self. That said, while I might argue An Apology for Poetry lacks contemporaneity, it still dedicates itself to maintaining the weight of art so it’s not lessened to desertion, and that is undeniably an initiative that deserves our attention. Artworks endure not because they remain unchanged, but because they continue to generate emotional, ethical, or political relevance as each generation discovers new ways to inhabit it. Though An Apology for Poetry is historically-outdated, its insistence on art continues to resonate. It compelled to write about it, like it compels one to understand art and poetry as an aid needed to enlarge our humanity.
